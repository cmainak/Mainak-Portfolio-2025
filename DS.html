THE PROBLEM!

After reviewing our extensive component library, we realized our design system had become fragmented. Components were scattered, documentation was inconsistent, and the designs didn’t match with what was live on the site. We needed a clearer, more consistent, and scalable foundation to support future growth.

RESEARCHING SYSTEMS!

To understand how to rebuild our fragmented foundations, we researched strong, well-established systems like Material Design, Carbon, Atlassian, and Dell. We wanted to understand what makes a system scalable, maintainable, and easy to govern across multiple products.

The strongest systems had 3 patterns in common:

  • Tokenized foundations instead of raw values
  • Component libraries built on a consistent structure
  • Clear separation between design intent and engineering implementation

Execution

METHOD FOR SCALE!

Token-First Approach

We rebuilt the foundations of our system using a token-first mindset. Instead of relying on raw hex values or inconsistent naming, we defined a semantic structure covering color, spacing, and typography.

✖ #684571 (Raw Hex)
✔ --purple-text-default (Token)

Custom Token Architecture

Instead of relying on plugin-based automation, we defined all tokens manually. This allowed us to create a well-structured, transparent system with precise naming conventions and semantic organization, giving the team confidence in its scalability.

Socializing The Update

To help teams adopt the new structure, we conducted demos and shared before-and-after comparisons.

OUTCOMES & INSIGHTS!

Once the new approach was shared with leadership, they requested an update to the entire component library. Thanks to our token-based foundations, the process was completed in about 10 minutes instead of taking several days.

FUTURE VISION!

Our long-term goal is to merge multiple UI libraries into a single, token-driven design system. This unified approach would provide a consistent foundation across all products, reduce duplication, and streamline collaboration between teams.

  • Shared foundation tokens for consistency
  • Primitives and components built on these foundations
  • Standardized icons, assets, and engineering handoff processes
