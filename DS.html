Researching Systems

To understand how to rebuild our fragmented foundations, we researched strong, well-established systems like Material Design, Carbon, Atlassian, and Dell. We wanted to understand what makes a system scalable, maintainable, and easy to govern across multiple products.

The strongest systems had three patterns in common:

  • Tokenized foundations instead of raw values
  • Component libraries built on a consistent structure
  • Clear separation between design intent and engineering implementation

Execution

Method for Scale

Token-First Approach

We rebuilt the foundations of our system using a token-first mindset. Instead of relying on raw hex values or inconsistent naming, we defined a semantic structure covering color, spacing, and typography. This ensured every component was tied back to meaningful, reusable tokens.

✖ #684571 (Raw Hex)
✔ --purple-text-default (Token)

Custom Token Architecture

Instead of relying on plugin-based automation, we defined all tokens manually. This allowed us to create a well-structured, transparent system with precise naming conventions and semantic organization, giving the team confidence in its scalability.

Socializing The Update

To help teams adopt the new token structure, we conducted internal demos, shared before-and-after comparisons, and developed migration documentation. These steps helped stakeholders understand the value of semantic foundations and how they would strengthen future component development.

Impact

Outcomes & Insights

Once the new approach was shared with leadership, they requested an update to the entire component library. Thanks to our token-based foundations, the process was completed in about 10 minutes instead of taking several days.

Key Insights

During the audit of the component library, We identified inconsistencies in naming, scattered documentation, and misalignment between Figma and production. To address this, we focused on establishing a clear, token-based foundation that would scale across products while giving the team full visibility and control.

Future Vision

Our long-term goal is to merge multiple UI libraries into a single, token-driven design system. This unified approach would provide a consistent foundation across all products, reduce duplication, and streamline collaboration between teams. Work on this vision is ongoing, ensuring it remains adaptable as the system evolves.

  • Shared foundation tokens for consistency
  • Primitives and components built on these foundations
  • Standardized icons, assets, and engineering handoff processes
