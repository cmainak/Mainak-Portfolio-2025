Bringing Structure to Chaos.

Building scalability and token-based foundations for a fragmented component library.

01. The Problem

After reviewing our extensive component library, we realized our design system had become highly fragmented. Components were scattered, documentation was inconsistent, and the designs didn’t match what was actually live on the site.

We needed a clearer, more consistent, and scalable foundation to support future growth and bridge the gap between design intent and engineering execution.

Key Friction Points

01 Scattered Components No single source of truth for designers or engineers.
02 Figma Misaligned with Production Hardcoded hex values and raw pixels meant updates in Figma didn't sync with code.
03 Outdated Documentation Lack of governance led to multiple versions of the same components.

02. Research & System Benchmarking

To understand how to rebuild our fragmented foundations, we researched strong, well-established systems like Material Design, Carbon, Atlassian, and Dell. The strongest systems shared three common traits:

01
Tokenized Foundations instead of raw hardcoded values.
02
Component Libraries built on a rigid, consistent internal structure.
03
Clear Separation between design intent and engineering implementation.

03. Building the Token Architecture

We rebuilt the foundations of our system using a token-first mindset. Instead of relying on raw hex values or inconsistent naming, we defined a semantic structure covering color, spacing, and typography.

04. Trial & Error (Iterations)

Instead of relying on plugin-based automation, we defined all tokens manually. This allowed us to iterate through multiple naming structures to create a transparent system with precise semantic organization.

05. Outcomes & Future Vision

The Impact: Once the new approach was shared with leadership, they requested an update to the entire component library. Thanks to our token-based foundations, the process was completed in about 10 minutes instead of taking several days of manual overrides.

Future Vision: Our long-term goal is to merge multiple UI libraries into a single, token-driven design system. This unified approach provides a consistent foundation across all products, reduces code duplication, and streamlines collaboration between UX and Engineering.
