PORTFOLIO REDESIGN

This self-initiated project was focused on rebranding and rebuilding my personal UX portfolio from scratch.

Timeline – Dec 2023 to Present

Tools – Figma, HTML, CSS, JS, GitHub, Netlify

Skills -

Responsive Web Design Bootstrap (Framework) Visual Design

BACKGROUND

When I first began building my portfolio, I did what most designers do: crafted it based on my own tastes, ideas, and assumptions. But halfway through my master’s program, a piece of advice hit me like a reality check:

"Your Portfolio is your work, but you are not the user"

That shifted everything. To redesign my portfolio through a user-centered lens, I had to start by understanding the actual users — recruiters and hiring managers — and their needs, expectations, and frustrations with design portfolios.

THE PROBLEM

My portfolio was built around my own preferences and assumptions, not around the actual users — recruiters and hiring managers. This made it harder for them to quickly understand my strengths and for me to stand out in a crowded job market.

🔷 🔷 DOUBLE DIAMOND DESIGN PROCESS

The project will follow a design thinking approach, closely aligned with the Double Diamond design process, to foster collaboration, innovation, and the fulfillment of user needs through continuous user involvement.

PHASE - 1 🔍 DISCOVER

📋 Research Objectives:

  • What key skills and qualities do hiring professionals want portfolios to showcase?
  • What do they find helpful or engaging in a portfolio — and what frustrates or turns them off?
  • How do they evaluate portfolios during the hiring process, and what sets strong ones apart?

📋 Literature Review:

  • Hiring managers spend less than 3 minutes on a portfolio during first screening.
  • They expect 3–4 strong projects with a visible, end-to-end process.
  • Storytelling, rationale, and visual clarity are highly valued.
  • Overly flashy or confusing layouts are a turn-off.

📋 Industry Analysis:

  • Improve scannability through better visual hierarchy.
  • Balance narrative and deliverables
  • Embed rationale directly into project sections, not hidden in text blocks

Main Themes Extracted from Hiring manager interviews:

...
  • “I want to see how they think — not just what they made.”
  • “A case study that clearly shows the why behind each step is 10x more impressive than a flashy UI.”
  • “Don’t make me work hard to find your best work — it should be obvious.”

PHASE - 2 👤 DEFINE

🧭 Who are we designing for?

While this project is technically about my portfolio, the real users isn't me — they’re the people reviewing it under time pressure, making quick-fit decisions:

  • UX Hiring Managers
  • Design Leads
  • Recruiters

Through interviews, literature reviews, and industry analysis, I discovered that portfolio reviewers typically spend less than 2–5 minutes on an initial pass. So my portfolio needs to communicate fast and clearly, while still allowing deeper exploration for those who dig in.

PHASE - 3 ✏️ DEVELOP



MID-FIDELITY WIREFRAME

✅ Usability Testing & Key Findings

To validate the high-fidelity prototype of my portfolio, I conducted a usability test with peers and industry professionals who had experience reviewing portfolios. The goal was to assess clarity, cognitive load, and navigational flow based on real user feedback.

🔍 Testing Goals

  • Assess overall clarity and content hierarchy of key sections
  • Identify cognitive load issues
  • Evaluate navigation (including jump links vs. separate pages)
  • Understand which areas felt redundant or unnecessary

    • 🧠 UX Principles Guiding the Design

    Informed by usability tests, I applied two key principles that directly address recruiter/hiring manager behavior:

    1. Cognitive Load Reduction

    • Visually scannable
    • Clear in hierarchy
    • Predictable in navigation
    • Light on jargon or filler

    2. Progressive Disclosure

    • Case study summaries show key outcomes, tags, and roles — click to expand full story
    • Process sections are chunked with headings, visual cues, and expandable modules
    • The About section leads with a short pitch, followed by deeper content (philosophy, timeline, resume)

    LOGO ITERATION



    When designing, the goal was to create a logo that visually communicated the app’s mission helping users make healthier food choices through simple scanning. Early sketches with food and barcode imagery felt too generic and failed to capture the app’s holistic nature.

    I pivoted to the concept of an “orbit” a circular symbol representing a complete, 360-degree understanding of product information paired with subtle barcode lines to reflect the scanning functionality.


    TYPOGRAPHY

    FINAL UI SCREENS

    ONBOARDING SCREENS

    Orbit Scan's onboarding introduces the app's core functionalities across four screens. It highlights instant health insights through product scanning, identification of harmful additives, and a reward system for making smart choices, encouraging users to begin.

    HOME SCREEN

    The Discover home screen presents users with personalized 'Products you may like' alongside 'E Gift Vouchers'. It also features an 'Articles' section offering informative content on healthy eating, packaging swaps, gut health, and clean bread options.

    SCANNER

    Left Screen: The left screen shows the detailed health information for a product after a scan. It highlights both positive and negative attributes of its ingredients.

    Right Screen: The right screen shows the options to "Add to Favorites", view the "Scoring method", and offers "Recommendations" for similar products.

    ADDITIVE ANALYSIS

    Left Screen: The left screen shows the detailed health information for a product after a scan. It highlights both positive and negative attributes of its ingredients.

    Right Screen: The right screen shows the options to "Add to Favorites", view the "Scoring method", and offers "Recommendations" for similar products.

    Before: The health bar was color-coded (green, yellow, red) to indicate product quality, but lacked any labels or context. Users were unsure what the colors meant or how to interpret the bar.
    After: We added intuitive icons (✔️, ⚠️, ❌) along with descriptive text labels like “Healthy,” “Moderate,” and “Avoid.” This provided immediate clarity on the product's health score.
    Reason: During usability testing, 4 out of 5 users expressed confusion about the color meaning. After adding visual indicators and text, comprehension increased by 60%.

    CATEGORIES

    The List of Categories screen allows users to easily browse top food product categories, such as Milk, Coffee, Juices, and Chocolates. Each category is visually supported with intuitive icons to enhance quick recognition and ease of navigation.

    The purpose of this screen is to streamline product discovery by organizing items into familiar categories, helping users quickly find and scan items of interest. This design supports faster decision-making while maintaining a clean and user-friendly browsing experience.

    REWARDS

    Left Screen: Users can explore featured rewards and browse categories like Retail, Health, and Fashion. This screen motivates users by showcasing a range of options they can unlock with their earned points.

    Right Screen: After selecting a reward, users choose the reward amount based on their available points. A clear, simple layout helps users quickly understand the redemption process and complete their reward claim with confidence.

    Result

    OrbitScan resulted in a fully designed and interactive high-fidelity prototype, showcasing a seamless barcode scanning experience and personalized nutritional insights. I prepared detailed screens, user flows, and export-ready assets to guide future development efforts.
