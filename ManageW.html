Manage Wise

Personalizing Productivity Through AI

ROLE: UX Researcher & Product Designer

TIMELINE: 10 Weeks

TOOLS: Figma, FigJam, Google Forms, Notion

TEAM: Solo (peer testers)

SCOPE: Concept-to-prototype of a productivity app with AI-assisted scheduling and goals

This project is a fictitious scenario, completed as a part of a graduate program. View High-Fidelity Prototype

Background

The productivity app space is crowded — Todoist, Trello, Microsoft To Do — yet users still struggle to manage overlapping tasks, track personal goals, and stay motivated. Through initial research and personal observation as an international student juggling studies, work, and personal life, I hypothesized:

“Users don’t need more tools. They need personalized support — one system that integrates tasks, goals, and progress insights without overwhelming them.”

Problem

Individuals feel overwhelmed by long task lists, struggle to prioritize, and juggle multiple tools (calendars, notes, to-dos), which fragments planning and reduces follow-through. The absence of a simple, systematic way to break down tasks and set priorities, plus distracted contexts, makes it hard to start—and even harder to stay on track.

Key Research Stats:

  • 70% of participants use at least one productivity app daily.
  • 40% still struggle with procrastination despite using these tools.
  • Most existing apps focus on tasks only, ignoring personalization and integrated goal-tracking.

    • How Might We

    “How might we design a personalized productivity app that integrates task management, goal-setting, and AI-driven smart scheduling — without overwhelming users?”

    This question guided my design process, focusing on creating a single, intuitive platform that adapts to each user’s unique needs and preferences.

    01 / Empathize – Exploring the User’s Needs

    Research Approach

    I conducted mixed-method research: 3 interviews, 2 surveys (10 participants each), and competitor benchmarking.

    Research goals:

    Key Findings from Customer Interviews

    Diverse goals, similar money struggles: Whether paying off student loans or saving for something big, every participant carefully managed finances between paychecks.

    Popular budgeting apps weren’t the answer: Most had tried Mint or Rocket Money, but quickly became frustrated by inaccurate categories, laggy updates, and needing to pay premium fees for full access. These tools felt complex or high-maintenance, and didn’t deliver what users wanted most: simplicity and reliability. Plus recent consumer apps like Surplus Expense Tracker show that people are already looking for ways to keep track of their leftover funds—proving this is a real need, not just a guess.

    Manual tracking was still king: Two participants opted for spreadsheets and handwritten logs, finding these more flexible and trustworthy than any app. It helped them break down their expenses and savings, tailored to their unique lives.

    Payment dates & automation struggles: Many wished managing payment due dates was easier. While some dreamed of autopay for everything, they worried it might mean less control or even unwanted overdrafts. The right balance of convenience and control was missing.

    Across the board, these young professionals wanted a banking tool that would do the heavy lifting for them—making it easy to spot extra cash in their accounts and turn it into progress with less effort, less guesswork, and fewer headaches.

    02 / Define – What Users Really Need From Surplus Manager

    Overview

    At this point, it’s time to put a name and face to the patterns we discovered. By bringing together real stories from our research, we create a clear picture of who we’re designing for—and the daily struggles they face when it comes to handling their leftover money.

    Meet Niya

    Thinking about all those conversations, a relatable story came forward: Niya, a young professional living on her own. She gets paid every other week, covers her rent and bills, and does her best to save—sometimes for a big goal, like grad school. But life is busy, and keeping up with budgets or extra money at the end of the month feels overwhelming.

    Niya has tested out a few finance apps, but most are either overwhelming or don’t sync smoothly with her Chase banking.. She wants one place, right in the app she already trusts, to quickly see what’s left in her account and easily put it towards her future—without the stress or spreadsheets.

    Goals

    • Build savings for grad school quickly and easily
    • Feel in control of “leftover” cash after monthly expenses
    • Automate progress—without overthinking or manual work

    Pain Points

    • Budgeting apps feel overwhelming or costly
    • Manual tools and spreadsheets take too much effort
    • Unsure how much is safe to save after bills
    • Worries about missing payments if she saves too much

    Behaviors

    • Checks Chase Mobile® frequently for balances & bills
    • Tried Mint and Rocket Money, but abandoned quickly
    • Uses Google Sheets monthly for tracking
    • Relies on app reviews and friends’ recommendations

    App Expectations

    • Clear and automatic view of surplus funds
    • Easy, flexible transfers to savings or investment
    • Guidance tailored to her habits—no nags or premium fees
    • All inside the Chase app she already trusts

    What’s Getting in the Way?

    So, after all the stories and notes, here’s what stood out: Niya isn’t alone. Many users like her feel unsure about their “extra” money and don’t have a simple, helpful way to save or invest it—right now, when it matters most.

    If we could wave a magic wand, what would truly help? These are the questions guiding our next steps:

    • How might we help Niya (and others like her) clearly see what’s actually left after bills—so she feels in control and confident about her money?
    • How might we make moving surplus money into savings or investment the easiest part of her month—not another chore?
    • How might we build the right habits, little by little—so Niya (and everyone like her) starts celebrating progress without the pressure?

    These honest, everyday questions become our roadmap. Everything we design for Surplus Manager will help users like Niya do more with the money they work so hard for—without extra stress.

    03 / Ideate – Creating the Framework

    How My Ideas Took Shape

    For the ideation phase, I utilized the Crazy 8 brainstorming exercise. Each session consisted of five minutes spent rapidly sketching as many ideas as possible—ideally eight per round—followed by an intentional 20–30 minute break. I didn’t always complete eight sketches each round, but this approach pushed me to generate a wide range of concepts in a short time and gave me space to reflect between sessions.

    After five Crazy 8 sessions, I compiled my sketches and organized them by theme. I filled pages with ideas, doodles, and quick notes. Then, I circled the ones that made me think, “Yes, this actually helps!” Every idea I kept was inspired by something users told me—they wanted things to be clear, quick, and give them a little nudge when they need it.

    04 / Prototype – Let's Make the Design!

    Wireframes

    While developing the storyboard concept of paying by paycheck, I examined Bank of America’s mobile banking app and noticed features like “Bill Pay” and “Life Plan” that had been available for years but were largely unnoticed by users. Even participants who were existing customers had never used them.

    I decided to integrate similar ideas into my finance management feature. Participants managed finances based on their paycheck, bills, and savings goals, so the new feature would allow them to set up their plan based on pay schedules (bi-weekly, monthly, or custom).

    The “Money Manager” feature appears on the dashboard and displays the available balance, scheduled bill payments, and remaining funds until the next paycheck. I also integrated goal tracking and easy access to upcoming payments.

    WIREFRAMES

    View Low-Fidelity Prototype

    Low-Fidelity Prototype Usability Testing

    I tested this prototype with 3 participants. Feedback revealed that some UI elements caused confusion, such as large negative balances in “Pending Transactions.” Based on insights, I iterated the flow to prioritize surplus balance management rather than individual payment flows.

    High-Fidelity Prototype

    Based on user feedback, I renamed the feature from “Money Manager” to “Surplus Manager” to better reflect its purpose. Participants found the previous name misleading as it sounded investment-related.

    Key Highlights

    • Surplus Spotlight: Central focus on remaining funds after bills.
    • Manage by Pay Period: Users can track spending across pay cycles.

    05 / Test – It’s Time to Test the Prototype!

    Usability Test Plan

    I conducted tests with 4 participants—2 of whom were part of the low-fidelity prototype testing. For high-fidelity testing, the platform did not allow Bank of America demo logins, so participants used screenshots and scenario-based tasks. I ensured the interface felt intuitive for both Bank of America users and non-users.

    Usability Test Findings

    Participants appreciated the feature, noting it would simplify financial tracking and make the experience engaging. Key feedback included:

    • “Select Amount” confused users—they thought it referred to paying from the Surplus Balance.
    • One participant assumed “Minimum Amount” meant “total due” rather than minimum payment.
    • The term “Open Balances” was unclear for some participants, but they liked the interactive chart after explanation.
    • Participants suggested integrating Outgoing Payments more closely with outstanding balance management.

    Priority Revisions

    Based on feedback, I created a new user flow focusing on the Outgoing Payments pathway. This approach offers a more direct route for paying down debt and provides better clarity during transactions.

    Next Steps
