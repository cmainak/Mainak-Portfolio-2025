01 / Discover

The 2-minute carton of milk

The problem started by watching a friend with a lactose intolerance scan a plant-based milk carton for 2 minutes, Googling ingredients mid-aisle, and still leaving unsure if it was safe.

65% of U.S. shoppers report difficulty interpreting food labels. 72% want clearer ingredient information. The FDA emphasized clearer nutrition labeling in 2024–25, validating the need at both regulatory and consumer levels.

1

Confusing Labels

Technical ingredient names and vague health claims create uncertainty at the shelf.

2

Hidden Additives

Shoppers Google unfamiliar ingredients mid-aisle, wasting time and adding stress.

3

Dietary Restrictions

No easy way to verify if plant-based or dairy-free claims are trustworthy without deep label reading.

4

Decision Fatigue

Comparing multiple products leads to analysis paralysis and suboptimal choices.

How I validated this at scale

To move beyond a single anecdote, I ran a 22-person survey (ages 19–52, 40% with dietary restrictions) recruited via Reddit nutrition forums and campus boards, followed by 4 semi-structured interviews with in-store shadowing, including a session at a Trader Joe’s dairy aisle. I then ran a competitive audit of Yuka, Fooducate, and Open Food Facts, and reviewed FDA and EU nutrition label guidelines.

Across 22 survey responses and 4 in-depth interviews, the same frustrations surfaced repeatedly.

One participant kept coming up in every synthesis session. Her story captured all three themes at once — and became the foundation of the primary persona.

03 / Develop

Ideation, Testing & Key Decisions

Minimum Viable Loop: Scan → Immediate health score + ingredient breakdown → Personalized swap suggestions. Drafted into mid-fidelity wireframes and tested with 4 target users (20–30 min sessions) before high-fidelity design.

Early Ideation

Exploring breadth over perfection in early ideation

Before committing to visual design, I generated dozens of mid-fidelity wireframes to explore layout, hierarchy, and flow. The goal was to map the entire experience from onboarding to the intelligent scanner and rewards without getting bogged down in aesthetics. By focusing purely on utility, I could quickly build a prototype to put in front of real users.

Usability testing revealed three major speed bumps

Moderated sessions with 4 target users (20–30 min each). Tasks: scan product, read rating, set food preferences.

Issue: Score hard to parse

"I see 41/100 — is that good or bad?"

Added color-coded badge + traffic-light bar showing safe/risky zones + High/Limited risk tags.

Issue: 30-second wait after scan

"Why do I land on a blank camera screen first?"

Rating now overlays the camera in <2s via progressive disclosure. Full details on swipe-up sheet.

Issue: Toggles felt tedious

"Too many switches, then I still have to hit Done."

Replaced with visual icon chips, multi-select in one tap, auto-save on close. 3 steps → 1.

Key Decisions & Critical Trade-offs

Decision 1: Overlay vs. Separate Screen

Constraint: Users waited up to 30 seconds for a separate screen to load after scanning.
Decision: Shifted to an instant overlay with progressive disclosure.
Result: Perceived wait time reduced to <2s. Users see pass/fail before lowering their phone.

Before: Camera opens → blank screen → wait 30s for product details.

→

After: Rating appears as an overlay in under 2s. Full details via swipe-up sheet.

Decision 2: Traffic Light vs. Numerical Score

Constraint: A score of "41/100" lacked context, Stephanie couldn’t tell if it was acceptable for her diet.
Trade-off: Add explanatory text vs. replace with traffic-light system + risk tags.
Result: 60% improvement in comprehension. Validated by FDA research on color-coded labeling reducing cognitive load.

Before: Raw numbers and basic lists required mental math.

→

After: Traffic-light color coding + risk tags (High, Limited) for instant comprehension.

Decision 3: Toggle Switches vs. Icon Chips

Constraint: Toggling preferences required flipping each switch then hitting "Done", 3 steps per item.
Decision: Replaced standard toggles with visual icon chips, auto-save on close.
Result: Multi-select in a single tap. Steps reduced from 3 to 1 per item. Aligns with WCAG 2.1 guideline 3.2.2.

Before: Standard list with toggles + "Done" button required 3 steps per preference.

→

After: Visual icon chips (Gluten-free, Vegan, Lactose-free…), single tap, auto-save.

Lessons Learned

This research experience deeply shaped the way I approach design. Listening to real stories taught me that surface level observations often miss the most meaningful insights. It strengthened my desire to always dig deeper to uncover the root causes behind user behaviors and emotions, not just the visible pain points. This mindset now drives every project I take on, pushing me to design with more empathy, intention, and clarity.
