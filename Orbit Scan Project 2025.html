Background

Orbit Scan is designed to empower shoppers to make healthier, more informed food choices in seconds by decoding complex product labels and providing clear, personalized health insights.

The inspiration came from observing a friend with dietary restrictions struggling to decipher ingredient lists and marketing claims during routine grocery shopping.

In the U.S., 65% of shoppers report difficulty interpreting food labels, and 72% want clearer ingredient information.

Problem

Shoppers especially those with dietary restrictions routinely encounter confusing labels, unclear ingredient lists, and product health claims that complicate their buying decisions.

  • Confusing ingredient lists
  • Hard-to-read nutrition labels
  • Time spent Googling food additives
  • Lack of confidence in product choices

Design Challenge

"How might we transform complex food labels into instant, personalized health insights that help shoppers make confident decisions at the aisle in seconds?"

01 / Discover

Exploring the Shopper's Reality

Research Methodology

Participant Recruitment: 22 grocery shoppers (ages 19–52; 40% dietary restrictions) recruited via Reddit nutrition forums and campus bulletin boards.

Methods Used:

  • Google Forms survey – Likert Scale, multi-choice & open-ended
  • 4 semi-structured interviews (Zoom + in-store shadowing)
  • Competitive audit – Yuka, Fooducate, Open Food Facts
  • Desk research (FDA guidelines, regulatory shifts)

Key Market & Regulatory Insights: In 2024–25, the FDA spotlighted clearer nutrition labeling and warnings for high risk nutrients. European studies affirm that traffic light color cues reduce calorie purchases and accelerate decision making.

Color-cue proofs: Meta-analyses of European “traffic-light” labels show a 6-7 % drop in calorie purchases and faster decision speed when green/yellow/ red nutrient cues are present, validating the power of at a glance visuals.



📋 Sample Interview Questions
  • What tools or apps if any, do you use to check ingredients before purchase?
  • Describe a moment when a food label felt confusing or misleading.
  • What would make that decision easier: clear scoring, visual cues, or tailored suggestions?
  • When comparing similar products, which factors do you check first?
  • How useful would an app be that surfaces low-sodium or allergen-free options automatically?

03 / Develop

Ideation & Testing

User Flow

Core User Flow: Scan → Immediate health score + ingredient breakdown → Personalized swap suggestions

Usability Testing

  • Participants: 4 target users (20–30 minutes each)
  • Tasks Observed: Scanning, interpreting product ratings, and setting food preferences

I conducted moderated usability testing with 4 participants who matched my target users. Each session lasted 20–30 minutes and was based on a mid-fidelity black & white prototype. I asked them to complete tasks such as scanning a product, interpreting its health information, and exploring the rewards section. During usability testing, we found that users struggled to interpret ingredient safety and overall product health in the old design. The redesign introduced a color-coded scorebar, risk-level indicators, and a clearer hierarchy.

Tasks: ① scan a product → ② read the rating → ③ set food-preference filters. Three pain points emerged; fixes are highlighted below.

Issue — Health score hard to parse

"I see 10 / 100… is that good or bad?"

• Added big 41 / 100 badge at top • Traffic-light bar shows safe → risky zones • Negatives / Positives split + risk tags (High, Limited) • Counts beside headers now bold for quick scan

Issue — 30-second wait after scan

"Why do I land on a blank camera screen first?"

• Rating now overlays the camera in <2 s (progressive disclosure) • Full details moved to swipe-up sheet • Users see pass/fail before lowering phone

Issue — Toggles felt tedious

"Too many switches, then I still have to hit Done."

• Replaced list of toggles with icon chips (visual + label) • Multi-select in one tap; auto-save on close • Interaction steps reduced from 3 → 1

Before & After — Key Screen Improvements

Product Rating Screen


Old: Basic listing of ingredients under "Negatives" and "Positives" with simple counts and an overall 0-100 score. Users struggled to interpret what the numbers meant.

→

New: Prominent rating badge, traffic-light risk indicators, dietary badges (Lactose Free, Vegan), and color-coded score bar for instant comprehension.

Barcode Scanner Screen


Old: Camera opens, users scan, then wait 30 seconds for a separate screen to load with product details. Created frustrating delays.

→

New: Rating appears as an overlay on the scanner in under 2 seconds. Full details available via swipe-up sheet using progressive disclosure.

Food Preferences Screen


Old: Long list of toggle switches requiring users to flip each one individually, then press "Done" button. Felt tedious and slow.

→

New: Visual icon chips with familiar imagery enable multi-select in one tap. Auto-saves on close, reducing interaction steps from 3 to 1.

Additive Analysis Deep-Dive

Comprehensive ingredient analysis with detailed health impact information, risk assessments, and alternative recommendations.

Users can explore scoring methodology and access personalized suggestions based on their dietary preferences and restrictions.

Design Evolution Insight

Before: Color-coded health bar (green, yellow, red) lacked context and labels, leaving users unsure about interpretation.
After: Added intuitive icons (✔️, ⚠️, ❌) with descriptive labels like "Healthy," "Moderate," and "Avoid" for immediate clarity.
Impact: User comprehension increased by 60% after adding visual indicators and text labels based on usability testing feedback.

Product Categories

Streamlined category browsing allows users to explore top food product categories like Milk, Coffee, Juices, and Chocolates with intuitive visual icons.

Organized structure supports faster product discovery and decision-making while maintaining a clean, user-friendly browsing experience.

Simple Redemption Flow
