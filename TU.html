The Story Behind the Project

When Green’s Blue Light Lounge was preparing to open in Manhattan's Upper West Side, they needed more than just a nice logo—they needed a distinct visual system that balanced the relaxed energy of a neighborhood lounge with the sleekness of a high-end cocktail spot.

My goal was to design a comprehensive brand identity system that could scale effortlessly. Whether rendered on a tiny mobile app screen, printed on a textured menu, or scaled up for a giant outdoor billboard, every piece of media had to feel unmistakably "GBLL."

How I Approached the Execution

1. Protecting the Logo with Clear Rules

A logo is only as strong as its consistency. I built a strict clear-space boundary (using the letter height 'B' as the unit of measure) so the logomark always gets breathing room. I also established clear "Do’s and Don’ts" to prevent accidental stretching, font swaps, or low-contrast placement.

2. Dialing in a High-Contrast Palette

To give the venue its signature nighttime aesthetic, I paired deep, dark greens and moody blacks with a vibrant electric neon accent (#9CF400). I documented exact RGB, HEX, and CMYK color values to guarantee the neon pops on screen and prints accurately in physical media.

3. Bringing the Brand to Life Across Touchpoints

Guidelines are only useful if they work in the real world. I translated the brand rules into tangible assets: custom food and drink menus, physical signboards, table tents, branded apparel, dinnerware, and intuitive mobile app mockups for reservations and ordering.
