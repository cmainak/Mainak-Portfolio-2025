Bringing clarity to complex food labels.

Nutri Scan was built to bring clarity to grocery shopping. It helps users navigate complex food labels through instant scanning and personalized health insights, turning 5-minute label reading sessions into 2-second glances.

01. The Problem

This project started in a grocery aisle. I watched a friend with lactose intolerance read a label, look up unfamiliar ingredients, and still leave unsure if the product was safe. Giving people a lot of information doesn't help if it isn't easy to understand.

According to FDA research, 65% of US shoppers struggle to interpret food labels, and 72% want clearer ingredient information. The technical names and vague health claims create uncertainty and decision fatigue at the shelf.

Identified Pain Points

01 Confusing Labels Technical ingredient names and vague health claims create uncertainty.
02 Hidden Additives Shoppers google unfamiliar ingredients mid-aisle, wasting time.
03 Decision Fatigue Comparing multiple products leads to analysis paralysis and suboptimal choices.

02. Strategy & Persona

I synthesized data from 22 surveys and 4 in-depth interviews. A clear business opportunity emerged: while competitors provided raw data, they hid actionable personalization (like allergy filters) behind paywalls. The goal became clear: How might we translate complex food labels into instant, personalized insights?

Primary Persona: Stephanie (24, Grad Student)

"I just want a quick Yes or No so I can get out of the aisle. Why is reading labels harder than my grad school textbooks?"

Goals
  • Get in and out in under 25 mins.
  • Know instantly if a product is truly safe.
Frustrations
  • Overwhelmed by label overload.
  • Distrusts unclear or "natural" claims.

03. Architecture & User Flow

To solve Stephanie's need for speed, the architecture had to do the heavy lifting in under 2 seconds. I mapped out a streamlined flow: Scan → Instant Health Score → Personalized Swap Suggestions.

04. Key Design Decisions

Decision 1: Progressive Disclosure over Separate Screens

In early wireframes, results loaded on a separate screen. Testing revealed users waited up to 30 seconds, causing friction. I rebuilt the scanner to overlay the health rating directly on the live camera feed in under 2 seconds.

Decision 2: Traffic Light vs. Numerical Score

Users were confused by numerical scores (e.g., "41/100"). Aligning with FDA research on cognitive load, I shifted to a color-coded traffic light system with plain-language risk tags.

Decision 3: List & Toggles vs. Visual Icon Grid

The original preferences screen used a plain checkbox list. Testing showed this was visually cold and mentally taxing. I replaced it with a scannable grid of branded icon chips and a thumb-friendly bottom-anchored Save button, drastically reducing recognition time.

05. The Final Prototype

The final interactive prototype successfully reduced time-on-task from over 5 minutes to under 2 minutes, achieving a 100% completion rate during moderated testing. Try the flow below.

06. Reflect

Testing the early prototypes completely broke my own assumptions. I originally designed flows around how I thought apps "should" work like moving a user to a new screen after scanning.

Watching users stumble over those decisions taught me that surface level logic often fails in practice. It reinforced my drive to prototype and test in realistic environments as early as possible, rather than waiting for a polished, but flawed, final product.
